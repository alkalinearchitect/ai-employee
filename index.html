What "managed" actually means

A managed AI agent is not a chatbot you configure. It is a deployed system with one job: fulfill the client's work without you in the loop. Dewey scopes the client, builds their agent from your proven playbook, wires it into their team's actual communication channel (Slack), and then absorbs the day-to-day support load. The owner's role becomes oversight, not operation.

The fulfillment architecture

The reason this model holds up at $5k/mo is the four-layer build:

1. Build. Dewey assembles the client's agent from your knowledge layer — not a generic template. One fact, one home.

2. Onboard. The agent ships into the client's own Slack. Their team talks to it where they already work.

3. Fulfill. The agent handles customer support and recurring tasks. Problems get solved without a human in the middle.

4. Payoff. You collect $5,000/month per client, hands-off, while the agent compounds your delivery capacity.

Frequently asked questions

What is a managed AI agent?

A managed AI agent is a deployed AI system that performs a defined body of work for a client on an ongoing basis, operated by the provider rather than the client. With Dewey, Human Architect builds, hosts, and maintains the agent; the client pays a flat monthly fee and receives fulfilled work — not a tool to figure out themselves.

How much does the AI Employee cost?

The Dewey managed-agent service is priced at $5,000 per client per month. That single fee covers building the client's bespoke agent, onboarding it into their Slack, and handling their ongoing customer-support fulfillment.

What does Dewey do after it is built?

After launch, Dewey handles the client's customer support and recurring operational tasks from inside their Slack. The client's team gets answers and completed work in the channel they already use; you monitor outcomes rather than doing the work.

Do I need to be technical to sell managed agents?

No. The offer is sold as a managed outcome. The technical fulfillment — building, onboarding, and maintaining the agent — is delivered by the system. Your job is to bring clients and oversee, not to write code.

How is this different from a chatbot or a no-code tool?

A chatbot waits for prompts. A no-code tool waits for you to build the flow. Dewey is none of that: it is a managed employee that owns a recurring outcome for a client, wired into their real workspace, for a recurring fee. The difference is accountability — it is paid to deliver, not to be available.

Who is this for?

Founders, agencies, and operators who want recurring revenue from AI without being trapped in delivery. It is also for businesses that need their own agent built, onboarded, and supported without hiring a technical team.
